THE CLASS: an arm satisfied by executable DATA that carries prose. b22's arm B6

THE CONCEPT OF A CHECK:

  For each arm that asserts a literal substring against output the suite
  PRODUCED by running the subject, ask how many distinct string literals in the
  subject's own source could contribute that substring. Two or more and the arm
  is AMBIGUOUS: it cannot attribute the presence of that text to the thing it
  is checking.

FOUR STEPS, CHEAP FORM FIRST. (1) Find the arms -- subjects the suite PRODUCED,
which is the OPPOSITE population to b23's file-derived sweep, so the two
together cover both halves. (2) Collect every string literal in the subject
module. (3) Count how many contain the asserted substring: ONE is attributable,
TWO OR MORE is AMBIGUOUS, ZERO is a third state meaning the substring is
assembled at runtime. (4) REPORT, NEVER FAIL -- ambiguity is not a defect, and
an arm asserting a phrase that appears in both a constant and a heading is
correct as written.

ON b22's OWN B6 THE CHEAPEST IMPLEMENTATION IS A HIT: "TWO EMPLOYERS" occurs in
the constant's note AND in the unknowns.push(...) argument. Two sources, one
arm, flagged.

WHAT IT CANNOT DO, named now rather than discovered later: a substring
assembled by concatenation has no single literal and reads as zero sources; it
cannot tell a REQUIRED duplication from an accidental one, so the static check
is TRIAGE and per-arm ablation is the VERDICT; and in a single-file app the
literal population is the whole file, which lowers precision.

NOT BUILT, AND ITEM 4 IS THE ARGUMENT RATHER THAN A PREFERENCE: the hard half
is finding the arms, and b24 measured that difficulty on the EASIER population
at ONE REAL FINDING IN FOUR FLAGGED. A B6 detector with worse precision than
that produces a work-list nobody reads twice, which is how a detector becomes
decoration. ROUTED FOR AN OWNER.
