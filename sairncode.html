Critical Care Transport

Two billing streams that must never be conflated: the air transport codes are billed by the transport supplier, the 994xx codes by the physician, often to a different payer. Every rule below shows the source it came from — check them yourself.
Codes recognised here: A0430, A0431 (air transport), A0435, A0436 (air mileage), 99466/99467 (physician face-to-face), 99485/99486 (physician supervisory), and the ventilation-management family for bundling checks. Ground ambulance codes are not included — they were not verified during this build and are absent rather than guessed.

Emergency Department

ED levels 99281–99285 are selected on medical decision making only — never time. Time belongs to critical care (99291/99292), where the clock pauses for separately billable procedures. Every rule below shows the source it came from.

Physical Therapy

The 8-minute rule aggregates the whole session first, then converts to units — counting each code independently is the classic over-bill. Every rule shows its source.

Mental Health & Counseling

Some limits here are licensure limits, not documentation ones — better notes cannot unlock a code the license does not permit. Every rule shows its source.

Urgent Care

The same visit is coded differently by payer — S9083/S9088 for plans that require the global code, standard E/M for Medicare, which does not recognise S-codes.

Optometry & Ophthalmology

The routine-versus-medical call comes first — it drives both the payer and the code family (92xxx eye visit vs 99xxx E/M). Refraction and materials stay off the medical claim.

Cosmetic & Reconstructive

The same CPT code is cosmetic or reconstructive based only on documented medical necessity. Cosmetic cases go to the direct-pay workflow, never the insurance A/R pipeline.

Drug Administration & J-Code Billing

Four things must line up on a drug claim or it is denied: the ICD-10 supporting medical necessity, the J-code (drug and quantity), the CPT administration code (how it was given), and the NDC. The tools below cover the three that are pure arithmetic or a mechanical rule; medical necessity is a documentation judgment and is deliberately not automated here.
1. Billing Unit Calculator
Units are counted by the code descriptor's billing unit, never per vial and never per order. Enter the billing unit from the descriptor itself (e.g. J1745 "Injection, infliximab, excludes biosimilar, 10 mg" → 10). For a single-dose container the billable amount is the labeled vial amount, not the dose, and the difference is billed as waste.
2. JW / JZ Waste Modifier Gate
3. Infusion Hierarchy — Which Code Is "Initial"
Only one initial service code may be reported per encounter. The hierarchy is chemotherapy > therapeutic/prophylactic/diagnostic > hydration, regardless of the order the services were actually given. Everything else on the encounter is a sequential, concurrent, or add-on code.
4. NDC Format Check
Claims need the 11-digit billing format in 5-4-2 configuration with no hyphens or spaces, preceded by the N4 qualifier, plus a unit-of-measure qualifier and quantity. Package labels often carry a 10-digit NDC in 4-4-2, 5-3-2, or 5-4-1 — a leading zero goes into the short segment to reach 5-4-2.
5. Biosimilar & NOC Code Traps
Two named denial triggers. Enter a HCPCS code to check it against both.

Which path is legal depends on the code's CMS PC/TC indicator, on who did what, and on whether the patient was a hospital patient — not on the code alone and not on specialty.

Privacy & Data Handling

Where your data goes

Entries you create in SAIRNcode (claims, denials, authorizations, eligibility history and the rest) are stored two places: in this browser, and — once you are signed in — on SAIRN's server, scoped to your practice's license so other practices cannot read them.

AI features and clinical notes

When you use an AI feature — the assistant, Suggest Codes From a Note, the rule-based scrub, or the 2026 code lookup — the text you submit is sent to Anthropic, which operates the Claude model, and is processed on their systems to produce the response. If you paste a clinical note, that note's contents go with it.

Only send what the task needs. A note stripped of the patient's name and identifiers usually produces the same coding answer as one that includes them.

The rule-based scrub and the 2026 code lookup additionally perform a live web search to ground their answers in current CMS/AMA guidance. The codes and terms you enter form the search query.

What is recorded about AI use

Every AI request is recorded in an audit log: who made it, which feature, when, how much text was submitted, and whether it succeeded. The note text, the prompt, and the AI's response are deliberately not stored in that log — recording them would create a larger, permanent copy of the very data the log exists to make accountable.

Third parties involved
  • Anthropic — processes AI requests, including any note text you submit.
  • Vercel — hosts this application and its API.
  • Supabase — stores your practice's saved records.
  • Stedi — only if your practice configures eligibility checks. Those requests go through your own Stedi account, not a SAIRN-owned one, so that relationship stays between your practice and Stedi.
Your control over this data

Every record has a delete control, and deletion is enforced server-side (a Compliance Admin session is required). Signing out clears your session from this device.

Stated honestly: there is no one-click "delete everything for this practice" function yet. Records persist until someone deletes them — see the retention setting below for what that means.

Data retention policy
Nothing is automatically deleted today. This setting records your practice's stated retention policy so it is written down and consistent. It does not purge, expire, or delete any record, and no scheduled deletion exists anywhere in SAIRNcode. Records persist until a person deletes them.

Medical billing records are generally subject to long retention obligations that vary by state, by payer contract, and by record type. SAIRNcode does not attempt to determine yours — set this to match the policy your practice has actually adopted.

The minimum is 10 years and cannot be set lower — enforced on the server, not just here. That floor exists precisely because no purge is built yet: whatever deletion mechanism is eventually built will inherit this value, long after anyone remembers choosing it.

Not legal advice

This page describes how the software behaves. It is not a HIPAA compliance opinion, and it does not replace your practice's own agreements with SAIRN or with any third party named above.
