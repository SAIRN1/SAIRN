1. ITEM 1 -- THE CAP GATE COVERED ONE VERB CLASS OF THIRTY-EIGHT
================================================================================

THE CONCEPT, STATED BEFORE THE CODE (item 5): a payload cap belongs to the
(resource, action) PAIR that writes, because WHICH VERB WRITES is a fact about
the handler and HOW BIG A ROW MAY BE is a fact about the table -- and the gate
asked NEITHER. It read `if (action === 'write')`.

This is cody's own b29 finding, closed here. 38 declared verbs across 77
resources bypassed the payload cap entirely. rf_invoices carries
rfinv_data_size <= 262144 and writes ONLY through issue / add_payment /
reconcile_claim / gl_export, so it was not under-capped, IT WAS UNCAPPED: a 10MB
payload reached Postgres and came back as an opaque constraint error, which is
the exact failure the MAX_PAYLOAD_BYTES comment records paying for on slabs.

WHAT CHANGED

  api/_resources/index.js   ACTION_CLASS, a declared map of all 38 verbs to
                            write-class or compute-only, with a LOAD-TIME
                            completeness assertion that THROWS and names any
                            unclassified verb.
  api/sd-data.js            the gate is now `if (action !== 'read')`, and the
                            cap resolves in three steps through one function,
                            payloadCapFor(): a (resource, action) entry, then
                            b28/b29's per-resource entry, then 64KB.

IT IS FAIL-CLOSED ON THE VERB, AND THAT ORDERING IS THE WHOLE SAFETY ARGUMENT.
The gate caps EVERY action except `read` rather than every action on a declared
write-class list. So a verb MISCLASSIFIED in ACTION_CLASS is still capped, and a
wrong entry there costs a comment rather than a hole. `read` is excluded because
a read payload is a FILTER: it writes no row, and capping it would be a new
restriction on something that was never the defect. Arm G4 asserts that
boundary, which is what stops the fix being widened past its reason.

STEP 2 IS WHY THE b28/b29 CAPS NOW REACH EVERY VERB. Those six resources had
their own caps and those applied only to `write`; a 300KB `agreement_status` on
rf_claim_agreements is now allowed by its 1572864 per-resource cap rather than
refused by the default. Arm G5.

AND THE ONE PLACE "THIS BREAKS NOTHING" FAILS IS rt_routes, WHICH IS WHY STEP 1
EXISTS. `upsert` and `optimize` carry the whole stop LIST, not one row:
api/_lib/routes.js permits 200 stops (:464) with 500-character addresses (:470),
so a full 200-stop route is 109KB MEASURED of legitimate payload and the 64KB
default would have refused it. THAT IS THE REGRESSION b29 DECLINED TO SHIP
BLIND, in its own words, and it is why that batch routed this item instead of
closing it.

The ceiling is DERIVED, not typed: RT_STOPS_MAX * RT_STOP_WORST_BYTES + 32768,
with both constants naming their source lines in api/_lib/routes.js -- the same
"one bound plus one stated allowance" form the dental photo-size migration uses
and defends. Arm R1 sends the real 200-stop payload; R2 proves the ceiling is a
CAP and not an exemption; R3 asserts `reorder` is deliberately absent, because
it carries a `seq` array of ids rather than stops and because "we forgot
reorder" and "reorder does not need it" look identical in a table.

THE CLASS MAP IS A DECLARATION, AND ARM M1 IS WHY THAT IS HONEST

The brief asked for the split into write-class and compute-only. The honest
first attempt was to DERIVE it: scan api/sd-data.js for each verb's
`action === '<verb>'` branch and test the span for a POST/PATCH/DELETE or an
/rpc/ call. THAT SCAN IS NOT TRUSTWORTHY AND THE MEASUREMENT SAID SO -- it
reported `consolidate` as having NO BRANCH AT ALL (0 found), and `reserve` and
`release_retainage` as compute-only, which is wrong for a verb whose whole job
is to reserve a slab. The span ends at the next `if (resource ===` boundary and
several branches put the write after a nested resource test.

So the class is declared by hand, and arm M1 RUNS THE REJECTED DERIVATION ANYWAY
and asserts the disagreements are EXACTLY the three pinned verbs. A new
disagreement means the scan has a new blind spot or a verb's class changed; a
disagreement that DISAPPEARS means the pin is stale. Neither can happen quietly,
and the gate does not depend on the declaration being right -- which is the
point of fail-closing on the verb.

re-run, so the figure is reproducible rather than a remembered first run).

THE CONCEPT: an append-only log is split on an ENTRY boundary, and the cut is
only safe if the entry SET is provably unchanged -- so it is verified by hashing
the entry multiset before and after, NEVER by comparing byte counts, because two
files that sum to the original size can still have lost one entry and gained a
duplicate of another.

`python tools/tooling_inventory.py --standing-doc-sizes` since b28 and recorded

              the ceiling. fourth hit the identical wall on 2026-10-12 and also
              went to two parts.
  attempt 2   N parts, treating everything before the first `## ` as a document
              header -- REFUSED on "not even one section fits the live file",
              because that "header" is 425,249 BYTES.
  attempt 3   three regions -- WRITTEN.

oldest 88 entries are bullets in the original format (423,683 bytes); the newer
127 are `## <date> (Cody) -- <subject>` sections (369,891 bytes). A script that
had GUESSED instead of refusing would have written a live file still over the
