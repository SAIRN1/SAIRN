4. ITEM 4 -- A COUNT MUST NAME THE COMMAND, AND THE LAST THREE RE-DERIVED

THE RULE, now in docs/METHODOLOGY.md: any count, range, total, percentage or
denominator in a report, digest, handoff or standing document CARRIES THE
COMMAND THAT PRODUCED IT, in the document, next to the figure -- not a
description of the method, the command, runnable as written.

WHY RULE E WAS NOT ENOUGH, measured on two of this platform's own figures:

    b9 reported 65 undriven suites; the real number was 36. The source was a
    census sentence in docs/known-red-suites.json saying "20 of the 86 entries
    ... were driven" while ITS OWN AUTHORITY LOG HELD 50 RECORDS -- and the
    same paragraph said "THE SWEEP LOG IS THE AUTHORITY, not this sentence". It
    NAMED THE AUTHORITY AND STILL PUBLISHED THE WRONG NUMBER, because naming a
    source is not naming a command. A reader cannot re-run a source.

    b10 reported 538 defect records; b11 re-derives 542. That one is NOT an
    error -- four landed from other sessions in between -- and it is the better
    illustration: a shared append-only ledger has NO STABLE TOTAL, so a figure
    quoted from it is only true as of a read, and the only honest way to
    publish one is to publish the read.

WHAT THE RULE BUYS THAT RULE E DOES NOT: RULE E makes a figure true at the
moment of writing. Naming the command makes it FALSIFIABLE AFTERWARDS, which is
the only property that matters, because every figure in a report is read later
than it was written. A reader with the command can disagree with me; a reader
with a prose source has to trust me, and three of my own published figures have
now been wrong.

NOT ACCEPTABLE AS "NAMING THE COMMAND", each of which has appeared in a SAIRN
document: "derived from the sweep log" (a source -- the exact wording that
produced the 65), "measured 2026-10-13" (a date is not a method), and a bare
figure with nothing beside it.
