SAIRNlearn — Parent Support
Ask me anything — how to talk to your child about mental health, warning signs to watch for, how to support them at home, or what to expect from the school process.
How to talk to your child tonight
Do: Find a calm, private moment. Start with "I have noticed you seem like you are carrying something heavy and I love you and I am here." Ask open questions — "What has been the hardest part lately?" Listen more than you talk. Tell them explicitly that nothing they say will make you love them less.

Do not: Do not start with "what is wrong with you." Do not minimize what they share. Do not immediately try to fix it — sometimes they need to be heard before they are ready for solutions.

If they say something scary: Stay calm. Tell them you are glad they told you. Call 988 together or take them to the nearest emergency room if you are worried about immediate safety.

Things that are true — even when it does not feel like it
💜 What you are feeling right now is real. And it will not feel this way forever.
💜 Asking for help is not weak. It is one of the bravest things a person can do.
💜 The adults working with you — your counselor and your parent — are not against you. They are for you.
💜 You are not a burden. The people who love you want to know when you are struggling.
💜 There is no feeling so dark that it cannot get better. But you have to let someone help.
What your counselor can and cannot share
Your counselor keeps most of what you share private. They will only tell your parent or another adult if they are worried you might hurt yourself or someone else — and even then, they will tell you first whenever possible. Their job is to support you, not report you.
