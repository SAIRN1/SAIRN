AI Assistant
Powered by Claude · Citation-grounded — never presents an unverified case citation or statute as fact
This assistant will never output a specific case citation, statute citation, or docket number in citable- reference format (e.g. "384 U.S. 436 (1966)") -- not even for a famous case, not even paired with a disclaimer. It can describe a case narratively (the holding, the parties, the era, the legal principle) but always directs you to Westlaw, Lexis, or the court's own site for the actual citation. It can look up this firm's real matter list and pending deadlines (matter number/name/client/practice area/status/attorney, deadline type/due date/ urgency) via tools, but has NO access to trust balances, invoices, time entries, matter notes, or deadline notes -- it will say so plainly and point you to the relevant panel rather than guess. Both are enforced by the system prompt below, not a UI label bolted onto unverified output.
Intake Memo Conflict Check Basics Deadline Reminder UTBMS Overview
Ask for drafting help, general legal-practice explanations, or this firm's real matters/deadlines. It cannot see trust balances, invoices, time entries, or notes -- check those panels directly.

Mock Trial
Adversarial stress-testing — an AI judge or opposing counsel attacking your position
This tool does not predict outcomes. It will never tell you your chances of winning, and any response containing a probability, percentage or odds is refused before you see it. There is no labelled corpus of case outcomes behind such a number, so it would be a fabricated figure dressed as analysis — and here it would be advising legal strategy. What this does instead is attack your position so you find the weak points before an actual adversary does.

Jury Assistance
Attitude-and-experience profiling, voir dire follow-ups, and Batson-defensible strike records
Race and gender are never a basis for a strike or a selection recommendation here. That is enforced in the tool, not just discouraged — a strike reason resting on either is refused, with the controlling authority shown. This also reflects what the jury-consulting field's own research finds: demographics are poor predictors of how someone votes, while attitudes, beliefs and lived experience predict considerably better. Demographics are recorded here as context only, never as the reason for anything.
Practice-juror difficulty
Training settings for the mock juror in a stress test. These describe how hard a practice audience should be — they are not a claim about any real panel.

Strike record (Batson / J.E.B.)
Records the real, case-relevant reason for a strike, tied to what the juror actually said. That anchor is what a Batson challenge turns on at step three.

Deadline Rules
Statutory deadline computation from a trigger event, showing the authority behind every date
No date is ever estimated. A deadline is computed only when the governing rule and the holiday calendar for every year the count crosses are both loaded. If either is missing, you get told exactly what is missing rather than an approximate date — a wrong deadline here is malpractice exposure, not an inconvenience. Every result shows the rule it came from and the steps it took to get there.
