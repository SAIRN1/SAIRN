## 6. ITEM 5 — `--reseat` NO LONGER REVERTS OVER SOMEBODY ELSE'S RECORD

**CONCEPT: a repair is judged on what it CHANGED, not on the state it
inherited.** `--reseat` ran `--check` after writing and reverted every re-seat on
any non-zero exit. Cody's `ce15ddaa6a3c` cites a sha that exists nowhere —
already on origin, caused by no re-seat and fixable by none — so `--reseat`
printed *"RE-SEAT REVERTED … nothing was kept"* **twice** in b27. **One session's
unresolvable record blocked every other session's correct repair, in every
clone.**

`--check` is now captured **before** the write as well as after, and
`_reseat_verdict()` decides on the **delta**: revert iff this run **introduced**
a problem. Pre-existing failures are kept and named as pre-existing, because
*"your repair broke the register"* and *"it was already broken"* are different
facts.

**The comparison is by PROBLEM, not by count** — a run that fixes one problem and
introduces another leaves the count unchanged, and a count-based test calls that
clean. **COULD NOT CHECK (exit 2) is still always a revert:** no evidence either
way, and PR §1.11 forbids folding that into a pass.

**Lock 29/29, up from 16.** Thirteen new arms including the b28 incident itself,
the 2026-10-07 defect it must not regress, the traded-problem case that defeats a
count, both could-not-check cases, and three on the problem-line parser the whole
comparison rests on.

**Driven live: three records re-seated AND KEPT** — `250099e90013`,
