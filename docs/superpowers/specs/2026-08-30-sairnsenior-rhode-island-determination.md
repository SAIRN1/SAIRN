# SAIRNsenior — Rhode Island determination: closed, and RI forecloses the Iowa model by statute

2026-08-30. **Scope determination**, same standard as the Iowa and OR/CO/NV ones.

> **A documented reading of primary text, not legal advice.** Confirm with counsel
> before anything customer-facing rests on it.

Round 18 left Rhode Island **not fully determined** — the only one of five in that
state — because § 17's *"arranges to provide, offers to provide"* language was the
widest trigger in the survey and because **Part 10, "Licensing Nursing Service
Agencies," had not been read.** Part 10 and its statutory definition are now read.

---

## The answer

**SAIRNsenior is OUTSIDE both Rhode Island categories.** Five determinations, five
states, all outside.

**And Rhode Island contains the single most consequential fact found about the
future marketplace question:** it **statutorily reclassifies** supplied temporary
workers as employees. An Iowa-style open-shift marketplace of independent
contractors is not merely licensable in Rhode Island — **its premise is
extinguished by statute.**

---

## 1. R.I. Gen. Laws § 23-17.7.1-2(d) — the definition, and the clause that matters

> "'**Nursing service agency**' is defined as any person, firm, partnership, or
> corporation doing business within the state that **supplies, on a temporary
> basis, registered nurses, licensed practical nurses, or nursing assistants** to
> a hospital, nursing home, or other facility requiring the services of those
> persons, **with the exception of hospitals, home nursing care providers, home
> care providers, and hospices licensed in this state.**
> **For all purposes a nursing service agency shall be considered an employer and
> those persons that it supplies on a temporary basis shall be considered
> employees and not independent contractors**, and the nursing service agency
> shall be subject to all state and federal laws which govern employer-employee
> relations."

*(P.L. 2005 ch. 127 § 2 and ch. 168 § 2; amended P.L. 2024 ch. 403 art. 2 § 10,
effective 2024-06-26.)*

**§ 23-17.7.1-2(c)** defines *"nursing assistant"* as *"a nursing, orderly, or
**home health aide** who is a paraprofessional trained to give personal care and
related health care and assistance"* — so the category reaches home health aides,
not only licensed nurses.

### Why the reclassification clause is the headline

Iowa's *health care technology platform* exists because independent contractors
**bid on open shifts**; Iowa § 135Q.1(7) even conditions the status on the worker
*"in the … professional's sole discretion"* choosing where, when and how often to
work. **Rhode Island says the opposite, for all purposes:** supply workers on a
temporary basis and **you are their employer and they are not independent
contractors.**

> **Consequence for the standing marketplace condition.** The four earlier
> determinations recorded that the triggers differ and *"would not all flip
> together"* if SAIRNsenior shipped open-shift claiming. **Rhode Island is
> stronger than a trigger.** A marketplace matching independent caregivers to RI
> facilities would not just risk needing a licence — the workers would be
> **statutorily deemed employees of whoever supplies them**, with all state and
> federal employer obligations attaching. That is a business-model question, not
> a compliance checkbox, and it belongs in the same decision-gate pass as the
> Iowa scope question.

> **WIDENED 2026-08-30, same day, by the reclassification sweep.** This document
> presents the clause as a *nursing service agency* rule. **It is not confined to
> that category.** The identical rule appears in **216-RICR-40-10-17** — the home
> care regulation — applying to *"a **home nursing care provider or home care
> provider**"*. Rhode Island applies it across its home-care licensing scheme, so
> an ordinary RI home care agency also cannot treat supplied caregivers as
> contractors. See `2026-08-30-sairnsenior-reclassification-sweep.md` § 1.

## 2. Why SAIRNsenior is outside Part 10

| Element of § 23-17.7.1-2(d) | SAIRNsenior |
|---|---|
| **supplies** RNs, LPNs or nursing assistants | **No.** It supplies software. It employs and furnishes no caregivers. |
| **on a temporary basis** | N/A — nothing is supplied. |
| **to a hospital, nursing home, or other facility** | N/A. |

**Outside on the first element**, which is the operative verb of the whole
definition.

**§ 10.3(A)(1) adds a holding-out limb**, as Oregon's caregiver registry does:

> "…no agency shall **hold itself or represent itself as a nursing service
> agency or use the term 'nursing service agency' or other similar term in its
> advertising, publicity or any other form of communication**, unless licensed…"

**Second state with a marketing-language trigger** (after Oregon ORS 443.100).
**Rhode Island marketing copy must not describe SAIRNsenior, or its customers'
use of it, as a nursing service agency or anything similar.**

## 3. Why SAIRNsenior is outside Part 17, now on firmer ground

Round 18 read § 17's *"provides, arranges to provide, offers to provide, or in
any other way provides for the delivery"* and called the margin narrow. **Part 10
resolves the ambiguity by showing where Rhode Island actually draws its line:**
the neighbouring category is defined by **supplying people**, and § 23-17.7.1-2(d)
expressly **excludes** licensed home nursing care providers, home care providers
and hospices from it.

Rhode Island therefore has a coherent scheme aimed at **entities that furnish or
deliver care personnel**, in two flavours — those who supply staff to facilities
(Part 10) and those who provide or arrange care in the home (Part 17). **A
software licensor that does neither sits outside both.** The verb list in § 17
reaches an entity that *arranges care for a client*; it does not reach a vendor
whose customer arranges the care.

**Stated as a limit, not smoothed over:** this is still a reading of two
definitions plus one part's general-requirements section, not of 81,660 +
28,769 characters in full. It is now **as well-founded as the Iowa
determination** and better founded than round 18's, but the surrounding parts
were not read.

## 4. Other Part 10 requirements worth carrying

- **§ 10.3(A)(2)** — *"A nursing service agency shall maintain a **licensed
  central office within the geographical boundaries of the state**."* A physical
  in-state presence requirement; compare Texas's prohibition on a virtual office.
- **§ 10.3(A)(3)** — **accreditation from an approved accreditation agency within
  twenty-four (24) months of initial licensure**, *maintained as a condition of
  licensure*. **Rhode Island now shows this in both parts** (Part 17 § 17 and
  Part 10), which makes it a Rhode Island policy rather than a one-off — and it
  remains the only state in the survey to mandate third-party accreditation.
- **§ 10.4** — a licence per **central office**; branches are subject to the Part
  but not the fee, and must be listed on the application. A **notarised list of
  direct and indirect owners with percentages** is required.
- **§ 10.3(B)** — exemptions under R.I. Gen. Laws §§ 23-17.7.1-13, -14 and -15,
  **but exempt agencies remain subject to the same nursing-assistant training
  requirements and duties.** *An exemption from licensure that is not an
  exemption from training — the same per-axis exemption shape as Oklahoma's DHS
  contractors.*

## 6. Method note

**Reading the neighbour resolved the ambiguity.** Part 17's verb list looked
alarmingly broad in isolation. Part 10 — read only because § 17 pointed at it —
showed the scheme's actual axis (*furnishing personnel or care*), which is what
made the Part 17 reading safe. **When a definition looks too broad, read the
category next to it before concluding.**
