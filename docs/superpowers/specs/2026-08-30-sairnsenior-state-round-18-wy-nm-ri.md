# SAIRNsenior — round 18: Wyoming, New Mexico, and Rhode Island opened

2026-08-30. **Research only.** Twenty-second document in the series.

Two routes solved by applying the South Carolina lesson (the numbering changed,
not the publisher) and one chapter found to be **an empty placeholder**.

**Rhode Island's licensure trigger is the broadest in the survey** — *"provides,
**arranges to provide, offers to provide**, or in any other way provides for the
delivery"* — and is the closest any state has come to sweeping a matching
business into its ordinary home care licence rather than a separate category.

## 1. Rhode Island — route solved, and a very wide definition

**Route:** Rhode Island recodified into the **RICR** scheme.
`rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/part/216-40-10-<nn>` returns full regulation text.
The relevant part is **216-RICR-40-10-17, *"Licensing Home Nursing Care Providers
and Home Care Providers"*** (81,660 characters).

> **Round 17 recorded Rhode Island as defeated by a soft-200** — every
> `webserver.rilin.state.ri.us/Statutes/...` path returns the same 1,676-character
> all-titles list. That host is still useless; **the regulations live on a
> different host under a different numbering scheme**, exactly the South Carolina
> shape. Found by enumerating part numbers and discarding the 7,418-byte
> not-found shell.

### Two categories in one part

**§ 17 definition 17 — "Home care provider":**

> "…any person that **provides, arranges to provide, offers to provide, or in any
> other way provides for the delivery** of any direct health care services in the
> home requiring supervision by a Registered Nurse (RN), but excludes the delivery
> of direct nursing care by a Registered (RN) or Licensed Practical (LPN) Nurse on
> an on-going basis; **Also as used in this Part, 'home care provider' includes
> homemaker services** … Nursing service agency services may also be provided
> under this license provided such services are rendered in accordance with
> **Part 10 of this Subchapter, Licensing Nursing Service Agencies.**"

**Definition 18 — "Home nursing care provider"** uses the same *"provides,
arranges to provide, offers to provide, or in any other way provides for the
delivery"* formula, for direct nursing services in the home.

> **"Arranges to provide" and "offers to provide" are inside the licensure
> trigger.** Every other state's matching category is a *separate* registration
> or licence with its own definition (Iowa's bidding marketplace, Colorado's
> fee-for-referral-only, Oregon's roster-given-to-clients). **Rhode Island appears
> instead to sweep arranging into the ordinary home care licence.**
>
> **SAIRNsenior's position, stated with its limits.** The verb list attaches to
> *providing for the delivery of direct health care services in the home*.
> SAIRNsenior licenses software to an agency; it does not arrange, offer, or
> provide for the delivery of care to any client. **On that reading it is
> outside** — but this is **the narrowest margin of the five determinations run
> so far**, it rests on one definition rather than a full reading of the part,
> and **§ 17's own text points at a further category (Part 10, Licensing Nursing
> Service Agencies) that has not been read at all.** Recorded as
> **not fully determined**, unlike Iowa, Oregon, Colorado and Nevada.

### What else the part requires

- **BCI check** — Bureau of Criminal Identification, per **R.I. Gen. Laws
  § 23-17-34**, with policies requiring *"timely completion"* and **"timely
  judgment regarding continued employment of an employee upon whom disqualifying
  information has been found."** *(Axis B, with an explicit duty to decide
  promptly — not merely to check.)*
- **Health screening and in-service education** under § 17.5.3. *(Axes D and A,
  not yet read.)*
- **Accreditation by a nationally recognized accrediting body must be achieved
  within two (2) years of initial licensure**, and maintained. **No other state
  in this survey mandates third-party accreditation.**
- **Contracted personnel must demonstrably meet the same requirements** —
  licensure, health screenings and criminal background checks — with documented
  evidence; another Department CHFR licence suffices as proof.
- The part sits inside a broad *"health care facility"* definition that expressly
  distinguishes **home nursing care providers** (skilled nursing, and optionally
  home care or nursing service agency activities) from **home care providers**
  (which *"may include services such as personal care or homemaker services"*).

## 2. Wyoming — home health agencies are licensed health care facilities

**W.S. 35-2-901(a)(x)** defines *"health care facility"* to include, among
sixteen categories, **home health agency** — alongside assisted living, adult day
care, adult foster care home, **alternative eldercare home**, boarding home,
hospice, nursing care facility and others.

**(a)(xi):**

> "'**Home health agency**' means an agency primarily engaged in **arranging and
> directly providing** nursing or other health care services to persons at their
> residence."

> **"Arranging" is inside Wyoming's definition too**, paired with *"and directly
> providing"* — so on its face Wyoming requires **both**, where Rhode Island's
> disjunctive list requires only one. Two states, two constructions of the same
> idea, and the conjunction is doing the work.

**Also found: W.S. 35-2-1201, "Senior health care districts."** A special
district may be established under the Special District Elections Act of 1994 to
deliver *"senior health care"* — health care for a person **at least 60**, a
**disabled adult at least 18**, or a person with medical or behavioural needs —
provided **through home care services**, assisted living, skilled nursing
facilities, nursing homes, hospice or residential care homes.

> **A public-financing structure, not a licensure category.** It is the second
> **age-scoped** trigger in the survey after Arkansas's 50-plus test, at a
> different threshold (60) and for a different purpose. Worth knowing because a
> Wyoming customer may be a **district**, not a private agency.

## 5. Method notes

- **Enumerate and discard the not-found shell.** Rhode Island's parts were found
  by walking `216-40-10-01…20` and treating every **7,418-byte** response as
  absent. The four real parts were obvious by size before any body was read.
- **A 403 on a directory listing is not a block on the content.** New Mexico's
  `/parts/title07/index.html` refuses directory access; the human-facing chapter
  URL resolves fine.
- **Four kinds of dead entry now seen:** repealed, renumbered, reserved-and-empty,
  and never-existed. Only the last is a bad citation; the other three are facts
  about the law worth recording.
- **Watch the conjunction.** Wyoming's *"arranging **and** directly providing"*
  and Rhode Island's *"provides, arranges to provide, **or** … offers"* differ by
  one word and produce very different scopes.
