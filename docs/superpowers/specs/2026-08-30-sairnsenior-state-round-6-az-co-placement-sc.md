# SAIRNsenior — state round 6: Arizona's disclosure regime, Colorado placement agencies, South Carolina

2026-08-30. **Research only.** Eighth document in the series.

The headline is not a new state. It is that **two of the three findings here are
about businesses that are barely regulated at all** — and both impose duties that
land directly on a scheduling product.

---

## 1. Arizona — the Administrative Code mattered less than I thought

Round 5 closed AAC R9-10 as unreachable after eight approaches and called
Arizona "statutory only", implying a gap. **Reading the two remaining statutes
changes that assessment: for the non-medical home care market, the statute *is*
the regime.**

### A.R.S. § 36-144 — Home care services; disclosure

This governs *"a business entity that provides home care services in this
state"* and requires an **annual disclosure to each home care service client** of
at least:

1. **Whether criminal background checks have been performed** on the employees or
   contractors who provide the services, **and the entity's policy on sending
   employees or contractors who have a criminal history into a client's home**;
2. the **name and position** of the person responsible for day-to-day management
   of employees and contractors, for hiring and firing, and for terminating
   contracts;
3. **a description of any required training** for employees or contractors,
   **and whether that training includes first aid and cardiopulmonary
   resuscitation**;
4. a description of the services provided **and the cost of each**;
5. a description of the entity's service agreements **and how an agreement may be
   terminated**.

"Home care services" means *"home care and support services including activities
of daily living and instrumental activities of daily living"* (§ 36-144(C)).

**§ 36-144(B) excludes** volunteers and family members receiving no more than
nominal benefit; **any organisation or individual licensed under title 32 or
title 36**; and anyone contracted through or on behalf of the state or federal
government.

> **This is a seventh shape for axis A, and it is the inverse of every other
> one.** Arizona sets **no training standard** for this segment — it requires the
> business to **publish what its standard is**, annually, per client, in writing,
> and to say **specifically whether first aid and CPR are included**. Maryland
> mandates CPR; Arizona mandates *disclosing whether you mandate CPR*.
>
> The modelling consequence is sharp: the compliance artefact is **a document
> generated per client per year**, whose content is drawn from the agency's own
> configuration. A product holding only "does this worker meet the state
> requirement?" cannot produce it. A product that already stores training
> policy, background-check policy, the responsible manager, the service list with
> prices and the agreement terms **can generate it outright** — and the exclusion
> at (B)(2) means it applies precisely to the unlicensed operators who have no
> other compliance scaffolding.

### A.R.S. § 36-425.01 — Home health agencies; licensure

The department licenses home health agencies by rule, for a period **not
exceeding two years**. **In lieu of a state licensure survey**, the department
*shall* issue a licence to an agency that applies and (1) is **Medicare
certified**, (2) has been **department-licensed for at least one year**, and (3)
meets all department licensure requirements. Full authority is retained.
"Home health agency" takes its meaning from § 36-151.

**A deemed-status route** — Medicare certification substitutes for the state
survey. Worth carrying because it means an Arizona agency's compliance posture
can be driven by CMS rather than by state inspection, and the two have different
cycles.

**Still unread: AAC R9-10** — the personnel and training rules for *licensed*
health care institutions. Closed, not pending; see round 5 for the ledger. Its
absence bounds the **licensed** side of Arizona only.

## 2. Colorado — Part 3, placement agencies, and a clause that constrains the product

Chapter 26 Part 3 registers a **home care placement agency** separately from a
licensed home care agency. Operating without registration is **a misdemeanour**
(fine $50–$500) **and** exposes the operator to a Department civil penalty of up
to **$10,000 per violation**.

**§ 3.2 criminal history — the same agency-judgment standard as § 5, plus five
enumerated factors.** The check must at minimum search Colorado criminal history
and be conducted **not more than ninety (90) days prior to placement**; cost may
fall on either party. In deciding whether to refer someone with a conviction the
agency **shall consider**: the history of convictions or pleas; the nature and
seriousness of the crimes; **the time elapsed**; **any mitigating
circumstances**; and **the nature of the position for which the provider would be
referred**. Policies and procedures are required.

> Note what that is: not a pass/fail gate but **a structured, five-factor,
> position-specific judgment that must be documented.** The same reasoning as
> § 5, made explicit. Any model that reduces Colorado to a boolean loses the
> whole rule.

**§ 3.3 disclosures — and this one bounds what a product may do.** The agency
must give the consumer a **written disclosure notice in the form and manner
prescribed by the Department**, **signed by the consumer or authorised
representative before services start**, stating at minimum:

> "(1) That the home care placement agency **is not the employer** of any provider
> it refers to a home care consumer; and (2) That the home care placement agency
> **does not direct, control, schedule, or train** any provider it refers."

**"Schedule" is in the list.** A Colorado-registered placement agency that used a
scheduling feature would be acting against the text of a disclosure its consumer
has signed. **For that customer segment, scheduling, training delivery and
assignment-direction are not features to be built carefully — they are features
that must not be offered at all**, and the product needs to know which
registration a customer holds before exposing them. Proof that disclosures were
provided must be retained before any referral.

**§ 3.4 inspections:** the Department may inspect records on weekdays 09:00–17:00
for compliance with the criminal-history, **general liability insurance** and
disclosure requirements; records retained **seven years** and readily available.
Consumer-home inspections require the consumer's consent.

**§ 3.6 intermediate restrictions** include retaining a consultant, Department
monitoring, **additional training for personnel, owners or operators**, a directed
written plan, or a civil fine up to **$10,000 per calendar year**; a written plan
is due **within ten calendar days** of a non-urgent notice, and an immediate
remedy is required for a serious and immediate threat, with written confirmation
inside **two business days**.

> **Two business models, one chapter, opposite obligations.** A licensed HCA
> (Parts 5–7) must train, evaluate competency, supervise and schedule. A
> registered placement agency (Part 3) must **not** direct, control, schedule or
> train — and must say so in writing before it starts. The five-axis model needs
> a **registration-category** dimension above the axes, because in Colorado the
> category inverts several of them.

## 3. South Carolina — statute read, regulation not located

**S.C. Code Title 44, Chapter 69 — "Licensure of Home Health Agencies Act"**
(1978 Act No. 548). Defines home health agency, home health services, branch
office, the Board and the Department (DHEC). Home health services means part-time
or intermittent **skilled nursing care** ordered by a physician, APRN
(§ 40-33-34(D)(2)(h)) or PA (§ 40-47-935(B)(8)) and under RN supervision, **plus
at least one other listed service** — therapy, medical social services, **home
health aide services**, supplies and appliances, or outpatient-arranged services.

**No training, competency or background-check requirement appears in the
chapter.** Same shape as South Carolina's neighbours in this survey: the statute
enables, the regulation specifies. **The regulation — DHEC R.61-77 — was not
located**, and is the real answer for South Carolina's axes A, B and D.

| Publisher | Result |

| `api.iga.in.gov` | returns JSON: **`"x-api-key not found"`** — a documented API behind a key, not a block |
| `rules.ok.gov` (Oklahoma Administrative Code) | **403** on the chapter and on the title |
| `doa.la.gov` (LAC Title 48) | 404 on every path tried; `ldh.la.gov` loads but is not the rule text |

**Indiana is a different category from the others and should be recorded as
such:** it is not refusing, it is asking for a credential and saying so, with
docs at `docs.api.iga.in.gov`. That is a decision to make (register for a key),
not an obstacle to work around.

## 5. What this round adds to the model

- **Axis A gains a seventh form: disclosure instead of standard** (AZ § 36-144).
  The state requires publication of the policy, not a floor.
- **A registration-category dimension sits above the five axes.** Colorado's
  placement agency and licensed HCA have partly *opposite* duties under one
  chapter. Florida's registration-vs-licensure split (round 2), Ohio's
  skilled-vs-nonmedical, Texas's seven categories and Colorado's Part 6/Part 7
  are all instances of the same thing: **the category determines which axes
  apply at all.**
- **Some compliance artefacts are documents, not statuses** — Arizona's annual
  per-client disclosure and Colorado's signed pre-service notice. Neither is
  answerable by "does this worker qualify?"
- **A negative capability is a real requirement.** Colorado forbids a placement
  agency from scheduling. That is the first rule found in this survey that a
  product must implement by **withholding** a feature.

## 7. Method notes

- **Closing a source is not the same as closing a question.** Arizona's AAC is
  still unreachable, but the two statutes read here answer more of Arizona's
  practical regime than R9-10 would have for the segment SAIRNsenior serves. When
  a source is blocked, ask what question it was for before recording the gap at
  full width — round 5 recorded Arizona as a larger hole than it is.
- **"403" and "needs a key" are different findings.** Indiana says so explicitly
  in JSON. Filing both under "blocked" would hide an available option.
- **Read the exclusion clause.** § 36-144(B)(2) excludes title 32/36 licensees —
  which is what makes the section apply to exactly the unlicensed operators who
  have no other compliance obligations, and therefore the most product-relevant
  part of Arizona's scheme rather than a marginal one.
