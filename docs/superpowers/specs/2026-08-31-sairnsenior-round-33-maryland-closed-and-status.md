## 1. Maryland's waiver: the client can override, their legal representative cannot

**COMAR 10.07.05.12D, Informed Consent** — read in full, and the distinction it
draws is one no other state in this survey makes:

- **§D(1)** — a competent adult client **or** a client representative with legal
  authority may consent to changes in the recommended plan of care.
- **§D(2)** — "**A cognitively capable adult client, but not a client
  representative with legal authority to make health care decisions,** may waive
  the licensee's recommendation of **certified care** services … to assist with
  treatments of a routine nature, or with the self-administration of
  medications."
- **§D(3)** — "A cognitively capable adult client may waive the licensee's
  **recommended skilled care.**"
- **§D(4)** — only after a **risks-and-benefits discussion**, captured on a signed,
  dated **informed consent form** in the record; and "**a form confirming a waiver
  of skilled services shall be signed by a cognitively capable adult client**."

> **The waiver that lets an uncertified worker serve a client is personal to the
> client.** A guardian, agent or health care proxy — the very people who
> ordinarily decide — **cannot exercise it.** Round 29 recorded Maryland's
> discriminator as "client condition with a consent override"; the override turns
> out to be narrower than that, and **capacity is part of the test.**

**And the supervision cadence is keyed to the medication relationship**
(§ .12E(2)): a registered nurse provides on-site supervision **at least every 45
days if staff administer medications**, **every 3 months if staff assist with
self-administration**, **every 4 months if neither**, or more often at the RN's
judgment given the client's condition.

## 2. Maryland's delegation rules: authorisation, stated as plainly as it gets

**COMAR 10.27.11** (Board of Nursing) is the mechanism that makes "certified
care" movable, and it is the clearest statement in the survey of the model
rounds 28–32 kept circling:

- **.03A** — a nurse may delegate a nursing task to **an unlicensed individual**, a
  certified nursing assistant, or a medication technician. **"The delegating nurse
  retains the accountability for the nursing task."**
- **.03C** — the task **"may not require the unlicensed individual … to exercise
  nursing judgment or intervention except in an emergency situation."**
- **.03D** — before delegating, the nurse shall assess the patient's needs,
  **"either instruct … or verify … competency,"** supervise, remain accountable,
  evaluate performance, and ensure accurate documentation.
- **.03E** — the nurse is **"the primary decision maker,"** operating within the
  employing facility's model of nursing practice, which must itself include
  mechanisms for identifying who may be delegated to and for **re-evaluating their
  competency**.
- **.04A** — the *degree* of supervision is set case by case from six factors:
  client stability, the delegatee's training, the nature of the task, **the
  delegatee's orientation to the specific patient environment**, their ability to
  perform it safely, and re-evaluation of the client's health status.
- **.04C–D** — on-site supervisory visits **every 45 days** for clients meeting the
  .03F criteria and **every 2 weeks** for those who do not.

> **Nothing here asks what the worker is.** It asks what the task is, who the
> client is, whether this nurse has verified this person for this task, and how
> often someone must come and look. **An unlicensed individual and a CNA appear in
> the same sentence throughout** — the credential changes what a nurse is likely
> to delegate, not whether delegation is the mechanism.

### Ten discriminators, confirmed with citations

| Discriminator | States | The question it asks |
|---|---|---|
| Worker class | Kansas | which class is this person in? |
| Task | Kansas 28-51-117(g), Maryland 10.27.11 | is this specific task permitted? |

## 4. Method notes

- **The stale-tab rule paid off twice more.** A screenshot timed out on the New
  Jersey tab; creating a fresh tab unfroze it. And clipped text was recovered by
  scrolling the document pane rather than by guessing the missing words — the

  another chapter.** COMAR 10.07.05.10D pointed at ".12D", and .12D pointed the
  substantive delegation question at 10.27.11 in a different title. **Three hops
  from the licensure chapter to the rule that actually decides who may do what.**
- **Nine rounds in one session, and the last state read produced the most
  decisive provision.** That is an argument about the remaining 38 states, not a
  boast about these twelve.
