# SAIRNsenior — round 16: Maine and Vermont, and a state where the market itself is closed

2026-08-30. **Research only.** Twentieth document in the series.

**TOC-first worked immediately.** Both states fell on the first properly-indexed
attempt after round 15 recorded that I had been guessing paths inside correctly
identified hosts.

The headline is Vermont: **home health service areas are exclusive franchises**,
and a 2024 statute lets an agency **refuse to dispatch a caregiver to a home**
where a previously-discharged individual is present.

## 1. Vermont — 33 V.S.A. ch. 63

### § 6304 — a geographic franchise, not a licence

> "The designation shall provide each designated agency **a franchise to provide
> home health services within one or more geographic service areas** within which
> it shall have **the obligation and responsibility of providing home health
> services for four years**… **No home health agency shall render home health
> services to patients residing in a designated service area without being
> designated by the Commissioner to serve patients in that service area.**"

Initial designations were written into the statute by name and county — e.g.
*"Addison County Home Health & Hospice, Inc.: the cities and towns of Addison
County, with the exception of Hancock and Granville."*

> **Vermont is a closed market, and it is the only one in this survey.**
> Everywhere else, licensure is a bar an applicant clears; in Vermont a
> **designation is exclusive to a territory** and carries a four-year *obligation
> to serve*, with initial areas anchored to existing certificate-of-need holders
> (18 V.S.A. ch. 221).
>
> Two consequences worth carrying:
> - **"Which agency serves this address" is a determinate, state-assigned
>   answer** in Vermont, not a competitive one.
> - **The addressable customer list is finite and enumerable** — a commercial
>   fact, not a compliance one.
>
> § 6307 leaves a narrow gate: the Commissioner **may contract with
> non-designated agencies** to establish *"specialized home health services
> needed but not available from the designated home health agencies."*

### § 6309 — staff safety, and a persistent client-level dispatch block

Added by **2023 No. 115 (Adj. Sess.) § 3, effective 2024-07-01**:

> "(a) If an individual was **previously discharged from service by a home health
> agency to protect the safety of staff** … and the behavior or conditions causing
> the discharge **cannot be reasonably mitigated or eliminated**, a home health
> agency may: (1) **deny a subsequent admission**; or (2) **decline to send a home
> health agency employee to make a visit if the home health agency has reason to
> believe that the individual who exhibited the behavior … is present in the
> home.**
> (b) Nothing … shall be construed to require a home health agency to enter a
> home to determine if a risk can be mitigated or eliminated.
> (c) A home health agency **shall provide notice of any denial of admission** …
> including **the reason** … and information regarding **how an individual may
> submit a complaint** pursuant to section 6308…"

> **This is a client-level safety flag that survives discharge and gates
> dispatch.** Note (2) precisely: the block is not on the *client* — it is on
> **sending a caregiver to a home where a particular individual is believed to be
> present**, which may be a household member rather than the client. That pairs
> exactly with **Oregon's "household individual"** definition (round 14) and
> Oregon's duty to push risk information to the assigned caregiver.
>
> **Two states, two legislatures, 2024 and 2025: caregiver safety is an emerging
> statutory dimension**, and both instances land on the *assignment*, not the
> client record. Any dispatch logic needs a reason-coded refusal path with a
> notice obligation attached.

### § 6321 — attendant care, and the client is the employer again

*"Recipients of attendant care services shall have the opportunity to **hire,
train, and terminate the employment of attendants** as necessary, **establish
work schedules, manage the services, and oversee payments** of attendants and
recordkeeping."*

Three named variants: **group-directed attendant care** (one or more attendants
serving a group of unrelated individuals in the same residence), **personal
services** (Medicaid-eligible elder or person with a disability, to avoid
institutionalisation), and **participant-directed attendant care** (permanent and
severe disability requiring help with at least two ADLs).

**Third self-direction model in the survey** after Nevada's client-as-managing-
employer branch and Oregon's agency-with-choice. In Vermont the client sets the
schedule and oversees payment and recordkeeping — so **the scheduling authority
is the client's**, which a product designed around agency-side scheduling
inverts.

### § 6308 — complaints, including from employees

Each designated agency must have grievance procedures allowing **recipients,
family members, and employees** to complain about access, cost, quality or other
operations; and DAIL must maintain an **external** complaint process with a
**dedicated toll-free consumer line**.

**Employees as complainants is unusual** — most complaint regimes in this survey
are consumer-facing only.

## 2. Maine — 22 M.R.S. ch. 419

Chapter structure (from the TOC): § 2141 purpose, § 2142 definitions, **§ 2143
license required**, § 2144 licensure procedures, **§ 2145 standards**, § 2146
fees, **§ 2147 exclusions**, § 2148 right of entry and inspection, § 2149
compensation for home health care providers, **§ 2149-A policies and procedures;
employment**, **§ 2149-B home health aide services to minors** *(effective on a
contingency — PL 2023 c. 412 Pt. GGG § 5)*, § 2150 compliance. §§ 2150-A and
2150-B are repealed.

### § 2149-A — the operative personnel section

> "1. **Policies and procedures.** A home health care provider shall develop and
> implement **written policies and procedures that prohibit abuse, neglect or
> misappropriation of a client's property.**
> 2. **Prohibited employment based on disqualifying offenses.** A home health care
> provider shall conduct a **comprehensive background check for direct access
> personnel**, as defined in **section 1717, subsection 1, paragraph A-2**, in
> accordance with state law and rules adopted by the department **and is subject
> to the employment restrictions set out in section 1812-G** … when hiring,
> employing or placing direct access personnel, **including a certified nursing
> assistant or a direct care worker.**"

**Axis B by double cross-reference** — the *definition* of who is covered sits in
§ 1717(1)(A-2), and the *disqualifying offences* sit in § 1812-G. Neither was
read. **The word "placing" is worth noting**: the duty attaches to hiring,
employing **or placing**, which reaches an agency that assigns without employing.

**Maine's chapter carries no training-hour requirement in its section list.**
Whether standards under § 2145 or department rules supply one was not
established.

> **A repealed chapter, recorded so it is not re-fetched:** 22 M.R.S. ch. 1625,
> *"Personal Care Assistance Services for Severely Physically Disabled Adults"*,
> is **entirely repealed** — §§ 7341, 7342 and 7343 all show (REPEALED). Round 15
> reached Maine's host on a wrong chapter (*Appointment of Receivers*); this is
> the second Maine chapter that looked relevant from a name and was not usable.

## 5. Method notes

- **TOC-first worked on the first attempt in both states.** Round 15's correction
  was the whole difference: Maine's chapter list named *"Chapter 419: HOME HEALTH
  SERVICES"* and Vermont's named *"Chapter 063: Home Care Programs"*, and neither
  would have been produced by a keyword sweep or a guessed citation.
- **Prefer a whole-chapter endpoint where one exists.** Vermont's
  `/statutes/fullchapter/33/063` returned 29 K characters in one request against
  a per-section crawl.
- **A chapter can be entirely repealed and still appear in the TOC.** Maine
  ch. 1625 lists three sections, all (REPEALED). The TOC tells you a chapter
  exists, not that it is live — the same lesson as *a name in a fee schedule is
  not a definition*, one level up.
