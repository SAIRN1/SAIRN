# SAIRNsenior — state round 12: Nevada, where the regulation says training time is paid

2026-08-30. **Research only.** Fourteenth document in the series.

Nevada's personal-care regulations contain the first rule in the survey that is a
**wage-and-hour obligation sitting inside a licensure regulation** — and a
self-directed model in which **the client trains the worker**.

---

## 1. Nevada — NAC 449, and it licenses the non-medical segment properly

Source: `leg.state.nv.us/NAC/NAC-449.html`, 1.6 M characters of Chapter 449.

**Nevada licenses "an agency to provide personal care services in the home" as
its own facility category**, listed in the fee schedule alongside hospitals,
home health agencies, nursing pools, referral agencies and *"employment agency to
provide nonmedical services"*. The substantive rules are **NAC 449.396–449.398x**,
with their own defined terms: *activities of daily living*, *agency*,
**attendant**, *client*, *personal care services*, *representative of the client*,
*service plan*, and **work station**.

**A fifth answer to the unlicensed-segment question.** Louisiana leaves it alone
unless bundled; Florida registers; Arizona requires disclosure; Arkansas trains
without licensing; **Nevada licenses it outright as a distinct category.**

### Axis A — NAC 449.3975 and 449.3977

**Qualifications (§ 449.3975).** Each attendant must: be **at least 18**; be
*"responsible and mature and have the personal qualities which will enable the
attendant to understand the problems of elderly persons and persons with
disabilities"*; **understand the provisions of this chapter and chapter 449 of
NRS**; demonstrate the ability to **read, write, speak and communicate
effectively with the clients**; demonstrate the ability to meet clients' needs;
and **receive annually not less than 8 hours of training** related to clients'
needs.

**Required knowledge and training (§ 449.3977).** Before providing services at
all, the attendant must obtain a working knowledge of the licensing provisions —
**and the agency must give them a copy**. Before providing services
*independently*, the attendant must complete a training programme that
**includes supervised on-the-job instruction with real clients**, during which
*"the administrator of the agency or the administrator's designee provides
supervision … to determine whether the attendant is able to provide personal care
services successfully and independently to the client."*

Training must cover:

1. **written documentation of services provided, and verification of time
   records**;
2. **client rights**, including confidentiality under state and federal
   regulations;
3. the **special needs of elderly persons and persons with disabilities**,
   including *"the sensory, physical and cognitive changes related to the aging
   process"*;
4. **communication skills** — active listening, problem solving, conflict
   resolution, and *"techniques for communicating through alternative modes with
   persons with communication or sensory impairments"*; and
5. **first aid and cardiopulmonary resuscitation**.

> **"Verification of time records" is a named training topic in a licensure
> regulation.** Nevada requires attendants to be trained in the accurate recording
> of their own time — which is the visit-verification discipline EVV exists to
> enforce, arriving from the state-licensure side rather than the Medicaid side.

**Second state to mandate CPR** after Maryland — and Nevada goes further: § 449.3976
requires proof, **within 6 months of starting**, of a **first aid and CPR
certificate** from the American National Red Cross or a Division-approved
equivalent.

### The finding that changes payroll, not just compliance — § 449.39735

> "…an agency shall: (a) Provide or arrange for the provision of the training…
> (b) …**pay any costs associated with attending such training**, including… the
> cost of the training; and if the training is not provided at the agency, **the
> costs of travelling to and from** the location…; and (c) **Pay an employee
> attending such training his or her salary or hourly wage as if the employee
> were working** for: (1) **Time spent attending the training**; and (2) **the
> time that would ordinarily be required to travel** from the agency to the
> location of the training and back."

Exceptions: an agency training **on its own premises** need not pay for
off-site training, provided its own meets the legal requirements and it pays all
associated costs; and it need not pay the costs for an employee who **attends but
fails to complete** the training. The agency may require receipts and proof of
mileage.

> **Every other state's training requirement is a compliance fact. Nevada's is
> also a payroll fact.** Training hours are **compensable at the employee's own
> rate**, and so is the round-trip travel time to off-site training. For a
> product that already holds shifts, rates and hours, the Nevada requirement is
> not a checkbox — it is **timesheet lines that must exist**, with a defined
> exception when the employee does not complete the course.
>
> This is the first place where axis A (training) and the wage/overtime research
> from `2026-08-29-sairnsenior-domestic-worker-overtime.md` meet in one rule.

### Axes B, C and D — § 449.3976's personnel file

A separate personnel file per attendant, containing at minimum: name, address and
telephone; **the date the attendant began working**; **documentation of the tests
or certificates required by NAC 441A.375** (Nevada's TB provisions — axis D by
cross-reference); **evidence that the references supplied were checked**;
**evidence of compliance with NRS 449.123** by the administrator or licensee with
respect to that attendant (axis B by cross-reference); the first aid/CPR proof
above; proof of age; and **proof of at least the minimum liability insurance
required by state law if the attendant will be providing transport**.

> **Transport is its own conditional requirement.** Whether the attendant needs
> insurance on file depends on **what tasks they are assigned** — the same
> per-assignment shape as Colorado's adaptive equipment and Oklahoma's task
> competency, applied to an insurance document rather than a skill.

### The self-directed branch — where the CLIENT is the trainer

Separately, NAC 449.395xx governs **intermediary service organizations** and
agencies providing personal care in the home, with a **"personal assistant"**
role: § 449.39517 qualifications and personnel files, § 449.39518 criminal history
reports, § 449.395185 performance of certain tasks, and —

> **§ 449.39519 — "Client to serve as managing employer and provide training to
> personal assistant; reporting of training to organization."**

**The client is the managing employer and the trainer, and the training is
reported to the organisation.** No other state read so far places the training
obligation on the person receiving care. For a product serving this segment the
training record is **submitted by the client**, not generated by the agency —
a different data flow entirely from every other state in the survey.

### Also present, unread

§ 449.39775 attendants' performance of certain tasks; **§ 449.3978 attendants:
prohibition on provision of certain types of services**; **§ 449.3979 written
disclosure statement to the client on acceptance for services**; § 449.3973
administrator qualifications; § 449.3974 personnel policies; § 449.3972 licensing
and **proof of liability coverage**.

**§ 449.3978 and § 449.3979 are the two to read next** — the first is a
negative-capability rule of the Colorado family, the second a disclosure artefact
of the Arizona family.

## 4. What this round adds to the model

- **A training requirement can carry a wage consequence.** Nevada § 449.39735
  makes training time and travel time **compensable at the employee's own rate**.
  Axis A and the payroll model are not separable in Nevada.
- **The client can be the trainer** (NV § 449.39519, self-directed branch), which
  inverts who produces the training record.
- **A personnel-file item can be conditional on assignment** — Nevada requires
  liability-insurance proof only **if the attendant will provide transport**.
- **"Verification of time records" is a trained competency**, not just a system
  feature.
- Nevada is the **fifth distinct answer** to the unlicensed-segment question:
  license it as its own category.

## 5. Method notes

- **Anchor a regex scan, and cap it.** A three-pattern scan over 1.6 M characters
  ran past the 120-second limit and had to finish in the background. Extract the
  chapter's contents list first, then jump to named sections — which is what
  produced everything useful here.
- **The body check is now earning its place.** It caught Connecticut's
  `200 + "Unable to Acccess Resource"` in the same pass that Alabama's `200 +
  404page` was still fresh. Two forms, two rounds, one check.
- **A section heading is evidence of a rule's existence, not of its content.**
  The client-as-trainer finding is labelled as resting on the heading, because
  that is all that was read.
