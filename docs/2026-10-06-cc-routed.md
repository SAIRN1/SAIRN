## 7. `docs/METHODOLOGY.md` — three conventions (fourth)

Each was paid for this batch.

**(a) A BRIEF'S PREMISES ARE VERIFIED BEFORE EXECUTION, WITH A PER-PREMISE RESULT
RECORDED.** Not "read the brief carefully" — *write down, premise by premise, what
you found at HEAD before doing any of it.* Measured: of the eight premises in this
batch's brief, **three were wrong about WHERE or WHETHER and one item was already
done.** The scratchpad a prior session was said to hold was empty; the pre-commit
fix was already committed; a test arm said to be missing was present and provable
by mutation; a tool said to exit 2 exited 2 for a different reason than stated. A
brief is the least trustworthy document in the repository precisely because it is
the most recent, and when it is *your own prior report* it carries your own blind
spots forward with full confidence.

**(b) A HOOK OR CHECKER LEG THAT RETURNS SUCCESS ON A FAILED LEG IS A SILENT SKIP
AND MUST FAIL LOUD.** The shape is `if rc != 0: return 0` — and the problem is not
the early return, it is that **nothing downstream can tell it from "nothing to
report"**. Three instances in this batch alone: the `--post` observer returned
silently when `rev-list` failed; `hover_routing_gap_check` returns zero gaps when
`routable` is renamed or a `ts` fails to parse; `mutation_anchor_check` reported
COULD NOT READ for five arms and exited 2 on a convention nobody had written down.
The rule: **every leg reports its own outcome and NAMES what it could not read.
Only a total blackout — no leg readable at all — is silence,** because then there
is no observation to offer.

**(c) WHEN ONE CHECKER PRODUCES THREE OR MORE FALSE-POSITIVE CLASSES FROM ITS OWN
AUTHOR'S COMMANDS, STOP PATCHING AND REVIEW THE DESIGN.** `exit_status_attributable`
reached **five** classes, and the fifth revealed that the defect was never in the
subject half at all — the same raw-text split was wrong in the *attribution* half
for every command carrying a quoted separator. Four patches had each fixed a
symptom. The design question — *should this read text or tokens?* — was only asked
at five, and asking it produced a better answer than another patch: a tokenised
parse agreed on all 23 arms and then **refused** an unmatched double quote the
regex handles, so it became a **cross-check** rather than a replacement, and every
arm is now decided twice by implementations sharing no code.

**The threshold is three because that is where a pattern becomes cheaper to fix
than to keep paying for**, and the signal is specifically *the author's own
commands* — a checker that fires on its author is firing on the usage it was
designed around.

* **STALE** — *"beacon published at 1 entries; log now has 19 (18 entries behind,

Plus MISMATCH ("the tamper-seal-broken case"), and COULD NOT RUN for both an

**SO THE SELFTEST HALF OF THE REQUEST IS ALREADY SATISFIED BY THE TOOL'S OWN
CONTROL.** What is genuinely missing is the `--max-lag N` parameter, so a caller
can state a bound instead of inheriting 15. **For hover:** that is a one-argument
change in your file, and the honest design note is that **15 is a reasoned number
and not a calibrated one** — the same disclosure `hover_routing_gap_check` makes
about its own 6h/15-entry thresholds. If `--max-lag` is added, the default should
stay 15 and the output should name which bound it used, so two runs at different
bounds are never indistinguishable.
