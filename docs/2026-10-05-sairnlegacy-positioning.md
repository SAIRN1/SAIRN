# SAIRNlegacy positioning — the breadth thesis is dead, and what replaces it is smaller

**Written 2026-10-05 (Fourth), from CC's
`docs/cloud-research/sairnlegacy-competitive-gap-audit-2026-10-05.md`.**
Nothing here is new research. This converts that audit's findings into what may
and may not be said to a prospect, in the same shape as
`docs/sairnlaw-deadline-engine-external-claim.md`, which did the same job after
Clio overtook SAIRNlaw's two headline claims.

**The app itself carries no overclaiming copy.** `sairnlegacy.html` was grepped
for `only platform / all-in-one / everything / unlike / no other / unified` and
every hit is a KPI label (`Not yet complete`, `Interment complete`) or a form
placeholder. **The overclaim was never shipped — it was in the planned pitch.**
That is the thing being corrected, before it reaches a deck.

---

## 1. The claim that has to go

**"One platform across cemetery, crematory, funeral home and livery — nobody
else bundles these."**

**That is false, and it is false in the easiest way for a prospect to check.**
CC's audit found four vendors marketing the same combination, several of them
in their own one-line descriptions:

| Vendor | How they describe themselves |
|---|---|
| PlotBox | *"Cemetery, Crematory, and Funeral …"* — verbatim |
| byondpro (OpusXenta) | *"a comprehensive cemetery, crematory, and funeral …"* |
| Cemetery Workstation | *"an all-in-one platform to handle all aspects of …"* |
| Halcyon (Batesville) | Cemetery Management, Cremation Management, … as named product lines |

**Breadth is the category definition, not a differentiator.** A buyer shopping
this market sees the same sentence on four other sites.

**And on one of the four businesses we are plausibly BEHIND.** PlotBox and
byondpro lead with **mapping and plot/record digitisation**. SAIRNlegacy's
cemetery surface is `leg_plots` and `leg_monuments` with **no mapping
anywhere**. Leading with breadth invites exactly the demo question we lose.

## 2. What is actually left, stated at the confidence it has

**Candidate differentiator, UNVERIFIED IN THE NEGATIVE: the livery/fleet and
events lines.** `leg_vehicles`, `leg_processions`, `leg_dispatches`,
`leg_maintenance`, and `leg_caterers`, `leg_florists`, `leg_liveryvendors` did
not appear in any competitor's advertised feature list CC read.

**Absence from four marketing pages is not absence from four products**, and CC
says so. So this is sayable as *"we have not found another vendor managing the
fleet and the event vendors in the same system — if yours does, show us"*, and
is **not** sayable as *"only we do this"*.

**Chain of custody: STILL UNKNOWN, and that is a result.** CC went looking
specifically because Part 1 nominated it as the likeliest advantage, and came
back inconclusive — no vendor advertises a first-class identification-and-custody
log, and "Cremation Management" is exactly the kind of label that could contain
one. **Settling it needs a demo or a customer, not another search.** Until then
it is a thing we do, not a thing only we do.

## 3. The constraint that matters more than any feature

**The category prices PER FIRM, $49–$200/month**, and vendors advertise the
absence of per-seat fees as a selling point (Gather; Osiris on *"affordable,
unlimited-user"*).

Our licence-key-per-tenant model is **aligned** with that — but the ceiling is
low and now known. **A death-care product competing on breadth is competing for
a $50–$200 seat, and 27 panels do not move that.** Any roadmap item justified by
"we can charge more for this" is assuming headroom the published prices do not
support.

**NOT CLAIMED:** that band is the independent-firm band. Multi-location groups
and mapped cemeteries — where PlotBox and Halcyon sit — publish nothing.

## 4. The disqualifier to fix before any pitch

**F1 is confirmed and is worse than Part 1 had it.** The FTC Funeral Rule
requires a written GPL on request, itemised casket and outer-burial-container
price lists, and a written statement of goods and services for every
arrangement — and the CPL has a **presentation trigger attached to the
merchandise screen the app already has**.

**Every established vendor sells this as a headline feature.** A prospect can
disqualify SAIRNlegacy in one question, and that question is the obvious one.
This is a build item, not a messaging item, and it outranks everything in §2.

## 5. The pitch, rewritten

> SAIRNlegacy runs a death-care business end to end — cases, dispositions,
> cemetery records, crematory operations, and the fleet and event vendors most
> systems leave to a spreadsheet. We price per firm, not per seat.

Then, in the demo, go at the **fleet and events** surface early, because it is
the part a competitor's demo may not have — and ask what they use for it today
rather than asserting they have nothing.

**What changed:** breadth moved from the headline to a subordinate clause, the
fleet/events line moved up, and no sentence claims to be alone in the market.

## 6. Never say

- ❌ **"Nobody else bundles cemetery, crematory and funeral."** Four vendors do,
  and two of them say it in their tagline.
- ❌ **Any exclusivity framing** — "the only platform", "no other vendor",
  "unmatched". Same rule SAIRNlaw adopted 2026-09-26, for the same reason: no
  audit supports one, and the single candidate differentiator is labelled a
  candidate.
- ❌ **"Full cemetery management"** while there is no mapping. PlotBox and
  byondpro lead with it; a prospect who has seen either will ask in the first
  five minutes.
- ❌ **"We have chain of custody that others don't."** Unknown, not favourable.
- ❌ **Funeral-Rule compliance, in any form, until §4 ships.** Claiming
  paperwork the app does not generate is the one overclaim here with an FTC
  enforcement action behind it.
- ❌ **Any price assumption above ~$200/month per firm** for the independent
  segment without new research.

## 7. What this document is not

It is **not** a verified feature comparison. CC's audit reads vendor
**marketing**, and a feature absent from a marketing page may be present in the
product. Every "they don't appear to" above is a prompt to ask, never a claim to
make. The two things that would actually settle §2 are a competitor demo and a
customer conversation.
