## 2. AI rate limiter

* **Ceiling:** `DEFAULT_DAILY_LIMIT` = **200 per 24 h sliding window** (was), env
  `SAIRN_AI_DAILY_LIMIT`. Tenant sub-budget and contention floor each default to
  **half** (100 / 100), binding only once an app is past the floor.
* **Keyed on:** the *budget app* — the verified licence's `app_id` (not the
  body's), or `anon:<app_id>` with no valid licence — and, inside it, the
  **licence hash** as the tenant. Not IP, not user.
* **Reachable by a normal day?** Yes for 200. Every tenant of an app shares one
  pool and one tenant is capped at 100 once the app passes 100 — a busy firm or a
  demo day crosses that in business hours. With the mode variable absent
  (Vercel production, per Michael) b25's resolver enforces it.
* **Set to 1000** (tenant share/floor → 500/500). **This number is a judgment,
  not a measurement:** no per-app daily count has been read from
  `sairn_ai_rate_limit_log`. Tune from the log or override `SAIRN_AI_DAILY_LIMIT`
  without a deploy. Tests: tenant arm re-aimed (100→500); **`ai-rate-limit.test.js`
  had been red since b25** (its observe arms relied on an unset mode meaning
  observe) — it now sets `observe` explicitly; 0 failed.
* **Not the same cap, not changed:** `DEMO_DAILY_LIMIT = 200` in `api/claude.js`
  (and `law-auth.js`) is an in-memory, per-instance, demo-only counter and answers
  `{error:'demo_limit'}`. A demo day on one warm instance can still hit it.
* **Burst:** not run. Unauthenticated calls answer 401 before the limiter, so a
  burst without a licence cannot reach it; a burst with one would **consume the
  shared daily pool of the licence's app for 24 h** in production. Needed: a
  licence for an app with no real tenants (or a preview deployment with its own
  Supabase), and a decision to spend that app's day. The cheaper route is the one
  b25 named: echo `rate_limit_mode` on the response.
