2. ITEM 1 -- --check PASSED ON A COMMIT THAT EXISTED IN ONE CLONE ONLY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT WAS WRONG, in one line of code:

    if git('rev-parse', '--verify', rec['commit'] + '^{commit}'):
        return rec['commit'], 'sha'          # accepted

`rev-parse --verify` accepts a DANGLING OBJECT for as long as the reflog holds
it. In b22 that is exactly what happened: a rebase replaced 634f7d70,
.githooks/post-rewrite reseated the three new records correctly IN THE WORKING
TREE, nothing committed the reseat, and the push landed the pre-rebase
citation. origin/main carried three records naming a commit that exists in no
clone but cc's own reflog -- and --check printed OK throughout.

THE OLD CONTRACT WAS TRUE OF ONE CLONE AND FALSE OF EVERY OTHER. Its stated
rationale -- "a record pointing at a real object is not a register pointing at
nothing" -- holds only where that object exists. The verdict depended on WHO
RAN IT, which is the one thing a shared register cannot afford.

THIS REVERSES A RECORDED DECISION AND SAYS SO. `reachable()` has carried this
sentence since 2026-09-16: "--check's acceptance contract is deliberately
unchanged." It is overturned in the docstring, on the evidence above, rather
than quietly contradicted by a later edit.

WHAT IS NOT CHANGED, because the 2026-09-11 reasoning behind it still stands.
An unreachable sha is NOT a failure on its own. It falls through to the
record's SUBJECT, exactly as an unresolvable one already did, and a unique
subject match is reported as a RESEAT. Rebasing is normal with five clones on
one branch and a register that goes red for it is a register somebody switches
off. The only records that now fail are those where neither a reachable sha
nor the subject answers.

MEASURED BEFORE THE CHANGE so the cost was known rather than hoped: of 653
non-external records, 648 resolve AND are ancestors, 0 resolved-but-dangling,
and 5 did not resolve at all and were already being re-seated by subject. A
zero is a weak test, which is why the reproduction below is DRIVEN rather than
inferred from it.

VERIFIED AGAINST THIS BATCH'S OWN THREE RECORDS, which is what item 1 asked
for. b22's three records put through both the pre-b23 and the post-b23

The first version used reseat_base(), which prefers origin/main and falls back

Because --add RECORDS A COMMIT THE MOMENT IT IS MADE, and that probe plants
records citing commits it creates in its own throwaway worktree. Those commits
are real, are on that clone's HEAD, and are not on origin/main yet. Refusing
them would make the register red for the entire normal window between
committing and pushing -- which is most of a batch.

still caught, measured rather than argued:

admits legitimate pre-push work and still refuses a reflog orphan, which is
exactly the line the b22 defect fell across.
