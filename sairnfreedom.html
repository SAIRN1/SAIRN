Foundation status for this post

What is built, and what is not

Phase 1 — Foundation: entity and licence model, officer capabilities, member roster, the three-layer fee structure and conditional waivers.
Phase 2 — Canteen & events: the POS with its members-only, permit-hours and staff-age gates (2.1), nightly close-out with cash reconciliation and the trustees' liquor inventory (2.2), automatic-only bottle fill determination (2.3), the events calendar — which is the same calendar as gaming, not a second one (2.4), hall rental with the three permit-modelled cases (2.5), vendors (2.6) and the two pricing capabilities plus the beverage cost audit that replaces shopping for alcohol (2.7).
Phase 3 — Gaming compliance: bingo accounts and the separate electronic instant bingo account (3.1, 3.2), the session model with calendar refusals (3.3), the profit chain (3.4) and operator eligibility (3.6). Item 3.5, the ORC 2915.10(A) record set, is deliberately held — it requires winner Social Security numbers and is being researched as its own task. This app does not yet produce a compliant 2915.10 record set.
Phase 4 — Disbursement & outreach: the disbursement log with two independent taxonomies (4.1), the ORC 2915.101 tiered calculation (4.2), funding source / game type / direction (4.3), donations with receipts that gate on 26 U.S.C. 170(c)(3) versus (c)(4) (4.4), cumulative-lifetime donor recognition with fulfilment state (4.5), outreach constrained to this organisation's own enumerated purposes (4.6) and service-hour tracking (4.7). Receipts are generated but nothing is emailed — there is no email endpoint for this app, and that is stated rather than implied.
Phase 5 — Members & operations: new-member onboarding through the same admission path the roster uses (5.1), volunteer e-sign bound to a document version and text hash (5.2), honour-guard equipment with shell-casing reconciliation and details shared across entities (5.3), post vehicles (5.4), a newsletter whose every number is checked back against the figures it was given (5.5), youth programmes named per order with photograph consent as a scope rather than a boolean (5.6), and veteran services for every order — referral, scheduling and accreditation only (5.7).
Phase 6 — District rollup: signed export and import of aggregate reports, including the compliance row. The live grant-gated cross-tenant read is deliberately not built — it needs a server tier and real authentication this app does not have, and a real district's use of the file exchange should inform that investment rather than the reverse.
Deferred, logged rather than built: building and grounds maintenance, officer election and bonding tracking, and the Phase 2 passive bottle-sensor hardware, which needs its own patent pass.

Initiation fee and annual dues are certified figures on a liquor-permit filing. District Rollup
Signed aggregate reports, exchanged as files — no live cross-tenant read exists, deliberately

This post's signing key

What the signature does and does not prove. The keypair is ECDSA P-256, generated in this browser; the private key never leaves it. A valid signature proves the report was produced by a browser holding this post's private key and has not been altered since. It does not prove the figures are true — the post computes its own report, which is correct because they are the post's books. Key distribution is trust-on-first-use: a district records a post's public key the first time it imports from it, and is warned loudly if that key ever changes.
Losing this browser loses the key. Storage here is local, so clearing site data destroys the private key and every future report will carry a new fingerprint that districts will flag as changed. Export the key below and keep it somewhere real before that matters.

Export this post's report

Aggregates only, by construction. The report contains no member, staff, operator, donor or winner names, no roster, no tickets and no prize list — those bytes are never placed in the document, rather than being filtered out of it later. That is the same reason ORC 2915.10(A)(3) winner Social Security numbers are held out of this app entirely: a district tier that never receives a thing cannot leak it.

Import reports from posts

Each file's signature is verified before anything is shown. A report that fails verification is rejected, not shown with a warning — an unverified figure in a district roll-up is worse than a missing one, because it looks like data.

Disbursements
Two regimes split by game type, and two taxonomies that do not derive from each other

ORC 2915.101 — the tiered distribution test

The threshold is configuration, not a constant. ORC 2915.101 states it as “two hundred fifty thousand dollars or the adjusted amount”, and the Attorney General may adjust it for changes in prices as measured by the consumer price index. Hardcoding $250,000 would silently go stale the first time it moved.

Record a disbursement

The two taxonomies are independent and neither derives from the other. The Ohio list measures where gaming net profit legally went and its content depends on organisation type — ORC 2915.01(V)(2) gives veterans organisations one enumerated list and (V)(3) gives fraternal organisations an entirely different one, inside the same state. The national category measures what the order's own reporting wants. A single shared enum would be wrong for one of them.

National program categories

Empty by default, on purpose. The five orders' national reporting categories differ and none of them was read against a real form for this build. Rather than ship an invented list that looks authoritative, this starts empty and you enter the categories your order actually uses.

Donations & Donors
Money received — the other direction entirely from disbursement

Record a donation

Recognition tiers

AtItemCostEmblemInsubstantial?
A tier is an award, not a disbursement. It sits on the money-received side and must never appear in the ORC 2915.01(V) taxonomy, which governs where gaming net profit goes. Two different directions of money; conflating them would put a donor pin in a charitable-purpose report. The pin's cost to the post is an expense on the other side of the books, and per the service-hour research “cost to post for X” is a legitimate national-reporting line but not a permitted (V)(2) destination for gaming net profit.

Donors — cumulative lifetime

DonorTypeLifetimeGiftsTier earnedFulfilment
Cumulative means lifetime, not annual. The running total survives every year boundary and every reporting-period reset, and it is rebuilt from the donation records on every render rather than stored as a number that can drift away from them.

Receipts

Nothing is emailed, and that is a real gap rather than a setting. The spec calls for automatic emailed tax receipts reusing the platform's Resend integration. There is no email endpoint for this app — the only two on the platform are app-specific and cron-only — and this app has no server table at all, so a receipt cannot be queued, sent or logged. The receipt is generated and shown for you to send yourself. Wiring the transport is real backend work, not a checkbox, and claiming it worked would be exactly the silent failure that left another app's reminders unsent for months.
Service Hours
Decimal hours, round-trip miles by occupants, and a reporting period that is configuration

Reporting period

Configuration, not a constant. The orders run four different fiscal years and none of them is shared. This build does not ship a per-order default because none was read against a real source — enter your order's own period.

Log service

Hours are stored as decimal — five minutes is 0.08, not 0:05 — because that is the unit the reporting forms use. Miles are round trip multiplied by occupants, which is how person-miles are counted, and is easy to under-report by a factor of the carpool.

Vendors & Pricing
What may be shopped for, what may only be audited, and why they are different

Vendors

VendorCategoryBrand / territoryNotes

Recorded prices — compare vendors already on file

This comparison is grounded entirely in prices the post entered. It is deterministic, auditable, and has no fabrication surface — every number came from a record somebody here wrote down. It needs no model at all, which is why it is a separate feature from the market search below rather than a narrower setting on it.

Search the wider market

Alcohol cannot be shopped for, and this is not a policy choice. Spirits are a state monopoly at a uniform statewide price. For beer and wine, OAC 4301:1-1-43(J)(2) prohibits a retail permit holder from purchasing a brand from a wholesaler whose exclusive territory does not cover the premises — so per brand the lodge has exactly one lawful vendor. Retailers may not buy from each other (4301:1-1-46(B)), discounts and rebates are banned both directions (4301:1-1-43(A)(2)), and beer and US-made wine must be sold to a retailer for cash (ORC 4301.24(D)). Buying outside that is a first-degree misdemeanor under ORC 4301.99(C) and reaches the permit through ORC 4301.25(A). The exposure is the lodge's, not ours — which is exactly why this app refuses the flow rather than warning about it.
Every price returned carries its source URL and the date it was read. A figure with no source is not shown at all. A searched price is a lead, not a quote: the Finance Officer budgeting against a stale scraped number is the failure this rule exists to prevent.

Scheduled sessions

Record session results

Game typeGross receiptsPrizes paid
Gross receipts is ORC 2915.01(T) — all money received without deducting prizes or expenses. Gross profit is 2915.01(EE), receipts minus prizes actually paid, and that is the figure 2915.10(C) requires be deposited. Depositing gross receipts instead is a records violation even when the arithmetic reconciles later.

What is deliberately not built here

Phase 3 item 3.5 — the ORC 2915.10(A) record set — is held, by decision, not by oversight. 2915.10(A)(3) requires storing winner Social Security numbers for prizes of $600 or more, and encryption, access role and retention are being decided as their own researched task rather than off the cuff. Held with it, because they belong to the same record set: the itemised prize list with winner name and address, participant count per session (A)(5), food and beverage sales (A)(6) and expenses (A)(7), the three-year retention rule, the annual supply inventory due 1 November (D), and notifying the Attorney General where records are kept (B). This app does not yet produce a compliant 2915.10 record set.
Bingo Accounts
Segregation is mandatory inbound; one enumerated expense class may cross outbound

Open an account

ORC 2915.10(C). The gross profit from each bingo session or game must be deposited into a checking account devoted exclusively to that session or game, and all bingo expenses and net-profit distributions must be made only by check or electronic fund transfer drawn on that account. There is deliberately no cash disbursement path in this app.

Accounts

AccountKindBank refDepositsPaymentsBalance

Record a payment

Only ORC 2915.01(GG)(11) has been read against primary source in this build. That division permits "expenses for maintaining and operating a charitable organization's facilities, including, but not limited to, a post home, club house, lounge, tavern, or canteen and any grounds attached" — which is why a canteen cost may lawfully be paid out of the segregated account. The other enumerated limbs of (GG) are unread. Anything recorded as "other allowable expense" is stored with your description and is not validated against the statute by this app.
Do not record a distribution of net profit here. ORC 2915.10(C) requires distributions to be drawn on this same account, so they will look like a payment — but a distribution is where net profit went, not an expense subtracted in arriving at it. Recording one here understates net profit and would feed a wrong figure to the ORC 2915.101 tiered-distribution calculation. Disbursement is Phase 4 and is not built; there is deliberately no category for it yet.
