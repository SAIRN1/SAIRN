I-9 Employment Eligibility Verification
Federal form — must be completed within 3 business days of hire
⬇️ Download Official I-9 PDF
⚠️ Federal Law: The I-9 must be completed within 3 business days of the employee's first day of work. Section 1 is completed BY THE EMPLOYEE on or before their first day. Section 2 is completed BY THE EMPLOYER within 3 business days. Penalties range from $272 to $2,701 per violation for first offense, up to $27,018 for repeat violations.
📋 I-9 Instructions for Stone Shop Owners

Step 1 — Employee completes Section 1 on or before Day 1. They enter their name, address, date of birth, Social Security number, and attest to their immigration status.


Step 2 — You examine original documents within 3 business days. You must physically examine the originals — photocopies are not acceptable. Record the document title, issuing authority, document number, and expiration date.


Acceptable Documents — List A (establishes both identity AND work authorization):
U.S. Passport or Passport Card · Permanent Resident Card (Green Card) · Employment Authorization Document (EAD)


OR — List B (identity) + List C (work authorization):
List B: State driver's license, state ID card, school ID with photo
List C: Social Security card, U.S. birth certificate, employment authorization document


Retention: Keep I-9 forms for 3 years after hire date OR 1 year after termination, whichever is later. Do not attach to personnel file — keep in a separate I-9 binder.

W-4 Employee's Withholding Certificate
Federal form — employee completes on first day
⬇️ Download Official W-4 PDF
📌 The W-4 tells you how much federal income tax to withhold from each paycheck. The employee completes this — you keep it on file and give it to your payroll provider. You are not required to submit it to the IRS unless specifically requested.
W-4 Key Points for Employers

• Employees complete a new W-4 whenever their withholding situation changes (marriage, new child, second job)

• Keep all W-4 forms for 4 years after the last payroll period to which they apply

• If an employee claims exempt from withholding, they must submit a new W-4 every February

• Use the withholding information to set up payroll — most payroll software accepts W-4 data directly

• New hires who don't submit a W-4 should be treated as Single with no adjustments

Silica Dust Hazard Training & Sign-Off
California SB 20 STOP Act — Annual training required by July 1, 2026
⚠️ California SB 20 (STOP Act): As of July 1, 2026, all stone fabrication shops in California must provide annual silica training to all employees who work with engineered stone. Engineered quartz is 93% crystalline silica — the most hazardous material in the stone industry. Silicosis is FATAL and IRREVERSIBLE. There is no cure.

CRYSTALLINE SILICA HAZARD COMMUNICATION & TRAINING RECORD

Annual Training — Required under OSHA 1910.1053 and California SB 20 (STOP Act)

What is Crystalline Silica?
Crystalline silica is a mineral found in stone, rock, sand, gravel, and clay. When stone is cut, ground, drilled, or polished, tiny particles of silica become airborne. These particles are so small they cannot be seen with the naked eye but are deadly when inhaled.

Silica content by material: Engineered quartz: 90–93% | Granite: 20–45% | Marble: <5% | Quartzite: 80–98%
Health Effects
OSHA Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL)
OSHA's PEL for respirable crystalline silica is 50 micrograms per cubic meter (50 µg/m³) as an 8-hour time-weighted average. Without controls, cutting engineered quartz can generate dust levels 100x–1000x this limit.

Shop Policies
Standard policies for stone fabrication shops — customize for your shop
📖 Recommended Shop Policies

Attendance & Punctuality — Stone shops run on tight installation schedules. Define: acceptable tardiness threshold, call-out procedure (who to call, how early), no-show policy, and pattern absenteeism consequences.


Cell Phone & Electronics — No cell phones while operating any power equipment. Define when phones are allowed (breaks, emergencies). Customer-facing staff need flexibility but shop floor is zero-tolerance during operations.


Vehicle Use Policy — If employees drive company vehicles for installations or deliveries: MVR check required, define who may drive, accident reporting procedure, personal use policy.


Customer Interaction — All pricing, discounts, and project changes must go through management. Employees should not quote prices, make promises, or negotiate with customers without authorization.


Social Media — No photos of customer homes, jobs in progress, or proprietary processes without written permission. No posting negative comments about customers, competitors, or the company.


Drug & Alcohol — Zero tolerance on the shop floor and when driving company vehicles. Define reasonable suspicion testing policy. Many workers comp policies require this.


Damage & Breakage — What happens when a slab breaks? Who is responsible? Define accident vs. negligence. Have a clear process: report immediately, document with photos, notify manager before customer is contacted.


Tool & Equipment Use — No personal projects on company equipment. No loaning company tools. Define who is responsible for tool calibration and maintenance reporting.


Dress Code — Steel-toe boots mandatory. Long pants required. No loose clothing near rotating equipment. High-vis vest on shop floor. Define what is and is not acceptable.
