Authorisations
What each payer approved, for what period, and how much of it is actually left — counted from real clocked visits, never stored
Authorisations are limited to management, coordinators and schedulers.
Authorisations and what is left of them
Delivered counts only real clocked time inside the authorisation period. Scheduled is future visits still booked against it, counted separately and never folded into delivered — merging them is how a screen reports room that has already been scheduled away. Remaining is against delivered only, so a cancelled visit cannot hand units back that were never spent. A past visit nobody clocked counts toward neither and is reported below.
Units are converted exactly from clocked time at the unit basis on each row. No state-specific per-visit rounding rule is applied — some programmes round down, some use an eight-minute rule, some bill exact, and guessing one would make every figure here quietly wrong wherever it does not apply. The service code is recorded and shown for the biller to cite; nothing is matched on it, because a visit carries no service code.

Pay Rates
What an hour costs — the other half of the branch margin, resolved against the date the work was done
Pay rates are the most sensitive record this app holds and are limited to management.
Rates on file
A visit is costed at the rate in force on the day it was worked, never today's — otherwise every historical margin would silently restate itself the next time anyone got a rise. Rates are keyed to the caregiver as the schedule knows them, because that is what a visit actually records.
Burden is employer payroll taxes, workers' compensation and benefits carried on top of the wage. It sits on each rate rather than on the agency because it differs by worker. Zero is allowed and means it is not modelled — it does not mean there is none, and where it is left at zero for a W-2 caregiver the branch margin is overstated by whatever the real burden is.

Add Pay Rate
The wage must be greater than zero — a zero wage would cost every visit at nothing while looking resolved, and report the branch as more profitable than it is. To supersede a rate, give the old one an end date and add a new one: nothing is deleted, because deleting it would leave every visit costed under it with no explanation and would silently change a margin already reported.
Franchise Royalties
What each unit owes for a stated period, computed from real claims — with what was left out, and why, printed beside it
Franchise agreements and royalty statements are limited to management.
Royalty statement
A denied claim is not revenue. The Branches rollup counts every non-draft claim as billed, which is right for an operational view — it was billed. It is wrong as a royalty base: charging a percentage of a claim the payer refused takes real cash for work nobody was paid for. Denied claims are shown here and charged to no one. A claim under appeal is in neither bucket — it was billed, then denied, and the outcome is undecided, so it is printed as its own in dispute figure rather than quietly counted or quietly dropped.
The royalty base is whatever each agreement says — billed or collected. There is no default: the two differ by months of cash and the difference favours one side of the agreement, so it is chosen per unit and printed on every row. The ad fund is computed on the same base and shown on its own line, never folded into the royalty.

EVV Configuration
Electronic Visit Verification is federally mandated (21st Century Cures Act) but each state chooses its own aggregator and format -- set this per your operating state(s), not hardcoded to one national standard.
This records which aggregator you are required to submit to. It does not submit anything. SAIRNsenior has no transmission path to Sandata, HHAeXchange, Tellus or CareBridge yet — visits are recorded here and still have to be submitted through your aggregator's own portal.
