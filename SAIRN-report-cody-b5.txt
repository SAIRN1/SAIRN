2. ITEM 1 -- THE LAW ROLE MATRIX, AND WHY IT IS ONE TABLE
================================================================================

BEFORE: exactly ONE role check existed across SAIRNlaw's whole data surface --
LAW_RECONCILE_ROLES, owner-only, and it was on the TWENTIETH resource. The other
19 were session-gated with no role distinction, so any signed-in role could read
and write law_picases (settlement figures, Tier A/A), law_pimedical (health
information about an identified person held by a law firm, Tier A/A) and
law_bankstatements (the bank side of an IOLTA three-way reconciliation) exactly
as freely as a managing attorney.

THE POLICY IS MICHAEL'S AND IS TRANSCRIBED, NOT DERIVED, and api/_lib/law-roles.js
says so at the top. b4 refused this item because api/sd-data.js declined to
invent the split TWICE IN WRITING -- ":13789 ... a product decision with no
obvious answer, and inventing one here would break the app for the people it is
for" and ":15719 ... a bar-rules judgement for the firm" -- and hover2 routed
the finding without prescribing a fix. b5 arrived with the decision made, so
this batch transcribes it. Changing it needs the same authority.

  owner, attorney        full read+write on all nineteen
  paralegal read+write   law_matters, law_picases, law_clients, law_matterdocs,
                         law_portalesign, law_deadlines, law_mattermilestones,
                         law_mattertasks, law_timeentries
  paralegal read only    law_invoices, law_opaccounts, law_optx, law_pimedical
  paralegal none         law_trusttx, law_bankstatements (TRUST-LOCKED),
                         law_portalmessages, law_barcerts, law_clecredits,
                         law_clerequirements

EVERY ROW CARRIES THE CATEGORY IT WAS PLACED IN. The policy arrived as
categories -- "cases, documents, calendar, contacts, tasks and time entries" --
and the mapping from a category to a resource NAME is the part a reviewer has to
be able to check. Six are denied to paralegal and FOUR of those are denied by
the STATED DEFAULT rather than by a judgment, which the row says rather than
leaving the reader to infer from an absence:

  law_portalmessages    FLAGGED IN THE TABLE as the most likely of the six to
                        need revisiting. Client CORRESPONDENCE is not
                        "documents" and not "contacts", so it falls outside
                        every named category. A paralegal who cannot read
                        messages on a matter they work may be operationally
                        wrong -- but widening it is the same kind of decision as
                        the rest of the table, so it is denied and flagged
                        rather than guessed into a category.
  law_barcerts          an attorney's bar admission record
  law_clecredits        an attorney's CLE credits
  law_clerequirements   CLE requirements per jurisdiction

TWO JUDGMENT CALLS INSIDE THE MAPPING, both stated in the code:
  law_portalesign  placed in "documents" -- an executed e-signature record IS a
                   document.
  law_opaccounts   placed in "billing" and read-only. These are the firm's
  law_optx         OPERATING account and its transactions, deliberately NOT a
                   trust account, which is the distinction the whole trust lock
                   rests on.

ONE TABLE, ONE FUNCTION, TWO CALL SITES -- AND THE TWO ARE NOT A SECOND COPY OF
THE POLICY. They are the gates that were ALREADY there: the SD_SESSION_GATED
block for the four resources named individually, and the LAW_RESOURCES prelude
for the fifteen in the dict. Each passes the same three arguments to the same
`decide`. Nineteen ifs is the shape being FIXED, not a style preference -- a
role check at one call site and not the others is indistinguishable from no
policy at all when read one branch at a time, which is how this app arrived
here.

THREE VERDICTS, NOT TWO, AND THAT IS WHAT MAKES IT SAFE TO PUT IN A SHARED
BLOCK. The SD_SESSION_GATED gate covers 70 action-pairs across the whole
platform. `decide` returns NOT_LAW for anything that is not one of the
nineteen, so a StoneDesk or SAIRNscape resource is neither allowed nor denied by
a law table -- only an explicit DENY stops a request. Folding NOT_LAW into ALLOW
would have made every non-law resource read as "the law policy approved it".

THE TRUST LOCK IS A SEPARATE STRUCTURE CONSULTED AFTER THE TABLE AGREES, and it
is the one design decision worth arguing. The table already denies paralegal on
both locked resources, so today the lock is redundant. It exists so it survives
somebody editing the table, or the per-firm override from item 4 being layered
on top. A policy expressed only as a default is a policy an override can erase.
Arm E drives it: the table is mutated to grant paralegal law_trusttx and
`decide` must STILL deny.

FAILS CLOSED IN FIVE DIRECTIONS, each driven by its own arm:
  - a law resource with no row DENIES, not "allowed by default" -- which is

  - an undeclared fourth role gets NOTHING rather than inheriting owner's access
  - a missing role in a row DENIES
  - any verb that is not 'read' is treated as WRITE-class, so a future
    soft_delete or export cannot ride in on a read grant (driven: law_pimedical
    read ALLOW, soft_delete DENY, export DENY)
  - a trust-locked resource DENIES regardless of the table

law_trust_reconcile IS DELIBERATELY ABSENT and `LAW_EXCLUDED` says so, with an
