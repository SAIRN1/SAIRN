Good Faith Estimates
No Surprises Act, 45 CFR 149.610 — the written estimate an uninsured or self-pay patient is federally entitled to, and the deadline it is owed by
This is a drafting and deadline tool, not legal advice, and it does not transmit anything. It covers only this practice's own expected charges: 45 CFR 149.610(c)(1)(iii) requires the itemised list to be grouped by provider and facility, and (b)(1)(v) requires a convening provider to contact co-providers within 1 business day. SAIRNdental does not gather co-provider estimates and does not pretend to — and whether HHS's 2022 enforcement discretion on co-provider charges still stands could not be confirmed against a primary source, so nothing here assumes it does.

Practice identity — required on every estimate
45 CFR 149.610(c)(1)(v) requires the name, National Provider Identifier and Tax Identification Number of each provider or facility on the estimate, plus the State and location where services are furnished. Without these an estimate is missing a required element, and this panel will say so rather than produce a document that looks finished.

Receivable ageing
Buckets are measured from each charge’s own date to today, and payments are applied oldest-charge-first because that is what an ageing report means — not because this practice has told us its allocation policy. A charge with no date is excluded and counted separately rather than dropped into 0–30. There is deliberately no industry benchmark on this card: the figures quoted in trade press for what a “normal” 90+ bucket looks like are not verified, and showing one would make a real number look good or bad against nothing.
